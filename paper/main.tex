% === Abstract ===
\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We present S(M,T), a unified scoring framework for routing queries to optimal language model endpoints. The framework combines multiplicative gating, weighted compatibility functions, and Boltzmann cost penalties into a single equation: $\smt = \left[\prod_j g_j(M,T)\right] \cdot \left[\sum_i w_i \cdot \phii(M,T)\right] \cdot e^{-\betavec(T) \cdot \mathbf{H}}$. The equation form is not new: under a log transform and softmax selection it is a constrained conditional logit (McFadden, 1974) with one log-sum compatibility regressor; our contribution is the parameterization of that form for heterogeneous endpoint routing, its analysis, and the empirical program. We prove convergence under Hajek conditions with logarithmic cooling, establish $O(\sqrt{KT\log T})$ regret bounds via contextual bandits reduction, and demonstrate weight identifiability when the phi matrix has full column rank. Grounding the framework against 2.76 million normalized benchmark records across 15,526 models from 7 public datasets, we discover a \textit{measurement gap}: 9 of 9 attempts to compute hand-designed phi functions from public benchmarks fail due to metric incomparability, domain transfer breakdown, and dimensional collapse. We report two complementary empirical results that point at this gap from different angles. \textbf{Benchmark result:} replacing the 16 hand-designed phis with 16 learned bilinear forms trained on 740,803 pairwise routing samples from 5 benchmark sources achieves 83.63\% accuracy on RouterBench (across 11 models) and AUC 0.8006 on RouteLLM (retaining 94.35\% of always-strong quality at 50\% cost). A scaling ablation (BilinearPhiEngine V3, 4.63$\times$ V1) confirms that data quality dominates model capacity for learned routing. \textbf{Production result:} a 61-day shadow deployment of the V1 scalar router with Robbins-Monro online learning across 1,580 routing decisions on a working developer's prompt stream achieves 94.7\% cost reduction against an always-Opus baseline, with 25.4\% of decisions routed to local agents rather than LLMs---the heterogeneous routing thesis realized as measured outcome. Offline replay of production outcome signals confirms that literature-prior phi functions (performance prediction, historical success, cascade position) collapse fastest under real feedback, while cross-node capability, complexity estimation, cost efficiency, and uncertainty signals concentrate. We refine the framework as V2 with two adversarially-verified additions: a headroom-capped composition operator restoring bounded scores, and a CVaR-based stochastic cost term necessary for agent-subprocess accounting.
\end{abstract}
